2. What Taskr Is, and How Pricing, Booking, and Payment Work

Taskr is an online marketplace that connects:

  • Seekers: people who post tasks (listings) they need done; and
  • Taskrs: people who request and perform those tasks.

Taskr is a platform. It is not the employer, contractor, or agent of Seekers or Taskrs, except as needed to process payments and hold funds in escrow as described in these Terms. Seekers and Taskrs contract with each other for the task. Taskr provides the tools, messaging, and payment processing.

2.1 Price types. Every listing uses one of two price types:

  • Fixed price: the Seeker sets the price. A Taskr can request the task only at that price and can’t propose a different one.
  • Negotiable: the Seeker leaves the price open. A Taskr who requests the task includes their price with the request.

There is no open bidding. Each request is for one task at one price.

2.2 Requesting a task. A Taskr requests a task by tapping Book. With the request, the Taskr proposes a date and start time for the job. The booking then shows as awaiting payment, and the listing is held for that Taskr while the request is pending. The work address stays hidden. We let the Seeker know in the app and by email, and ask them to pay to confirm. Taskrs never pay to book a task. Before requesting a task, a Taskr must finish payout setup with Stripe Connect, because the Seeker’s payment goes to the Taskr’s payout account.

2.3 The Seeker confirms by paying. The Seeker always pays for the task. To confirm, the Seeker pays the requested price in the app through Stripe. On a negotiable task, paying the Taskr’s price means the Seeker agrees to that price. When payment succeeds:

  • the booking is confirmed;
  • the payment is held in escrow;
  • the Taskr’s proposed date and start time becomes the booking’s scheduled start (Section 2.5); and
  • the work address is shared with the Taskr.

2.4 Withdrawing, declining, and expiry. Until the Seeker pays:

  • the Taskr can withdraw the request at any time;
  • the Seeker can decline the request; and
  • if the Seeker doesn’t pay within 24 hours, the request expires.

In each case, the request ends, no one is charged, and the listing goes back on the board.

2.5 Start time, escrow, and release. When a Taskr requests a task, they propose a date and start time. When the Seeker pays to confirm (Section 2.3), they accept that time, and it becomes the booking’s scheduled start. Start times are shown in your local time (Alaska Time for Alaska bookings). The scheduled start can’t be changed after payment (Section 8).

The Seeker is charged when they pay, and the payment is held in escrow until the job is confirmed done. When both the Seeker and the Taskr confirm the job is complete, the payment is released 48 hours later, unless either party opens a dispute during that window. On release, the Taskr receives 90% and Taskr keeps a 10% platform fee (Section 2.6).

If one party confirms the job is complete and the other party doesn’t confirm or open a dispute within 48 hours, the job is treated as confirmed. The 48-hour dispute window then begins, and if no one opens a dispute, the payment is released to the Taskr. If neither party confirms, the payment stays held until Taskr support resolves it.

Disputes. Either party can open a dispute in the app any time after the Seeker pays, until the payment is released. You don’t have to confirm the job is complete first. While a dispute is open, the held payment is not released (Section 7). After the payment is released, a dispute can’t be opened in the app. Email support@taskrak.com instead.

Frozen funds. If a card chargeback is filed on a booking, or a Taskr’s account is banned, any held payment that hasn’t been released yet is frozen while we review it. When a Taskr is banned, any listing they were booked on goes back on the board as Open, and their open requests are withdrawn.

Banned Seekers. If a Seeker’s account is banned, each of their paid bookings is handled this way:

  • if the Taskr hasn’t marked the job as started, the booking is cancelled and the Seeker gets a full refund, with no late fee;
  • if the job has started and the Seeker hasn’t confirmed it complete (including when only the Taskr has confirmed), the held payment is frozen and Taskr support decides whether to refund the Seeker or release the payment to the Taskr (Section 7); and
  • if the Seeker has confirmed the job complete (alone or together with the Taskr), or the job is treated as confirmed under this section, the payment is released to the Taskr as usual.

The banned Seeker’s listings close.

Cancellations, late cancellations, and no-shows are covered in Section 8. We also email both parties about booking events, such as a new request, a confirmed booking, a cancellation, a refund, or a dispute.

2.6 Payout and platform fee. When the payment is released, the Taskr receives 90% of the task price through Stripe Connect, and Taskr keeps a 10% platform fee. If a Seeker cancels late, the late cancellation fee is split the same way: 90% to the Taskr and 10% to Taskr (Section 8). To receive payouts, the Taskr must complete Stripe Connect setup. Exact amounts are shown in the app before the Seeker pays.

2.7 Tips. After the payment is released, the Seeker can choose to tip the Taskr in the app. Tips are paid by the Seeker, go 100% to the Taskr (Taskr takes no fee on tips), and are optional.

Payments, escrow, payouts, and tips are processed by Stripe and are also covered by Section 5 and Stripe’s terms.

Payments are processed by Stripe (including Stripe Connect for Taskr payouts). By using paid features you also agree to applicable Stripe terms.

5.1 Escrow and split (platform fee)

  1. The Seeker’s payment is charged when the Seeker pays to confirm a request (Section 2.3) and is held in escrow via Stripe until it is released (Section 5.2) or refunded.
  2. On release, the task price is split:
    • 90% to the Taskr (payout via Stripe Connect); and
    • a 10% platform fee to Arctic Autonomy Ventures LLC d/b/a Taskr.
    Amounts are rounded to the cent and shown in the app before the Seeker pays. If a Seeker cancels late, the late cancellation fee is split the same way: 90% to the Taskr and 10% to Taskr (Section 8).
  3. Exact amounts, timing, and currency are shown in-app. Taxes (if any) are your responsibility unless we state otherwise in writing.

5.2 Dual confirmation and release

  1. Completion requires dual confirmation: the Seeker and the Taskr both confirm the work is done.
  2. The payment is released 48 hours after both confirm (or after the job is treated as confirmed under Section 2.5), unless either party opens a dispute during that window (Section 7). Payment isn’t released while a dispute is open, or while funds are frozen after a chargeback or a Taskr ban (Section 2.5).
  3. After release, escrow is paid out per the split above. We may adjust release timing with notice in the app.

5.3 Tips

  1. After escrow release, Seekers may optionally tip the Taskr.
  2. Tips are paid 100% to the Taskr (platform fee 0% on tips), subject to Stripe processing and Taskr Connect payout readiness.
  3. Tips are voluntary and generally non-refundable once paid, except as required by law or a successful dispute determination.

5.4 Failed or incomplete payouts

Taskrs must finish Stripe Connect payout setup before requesting a task. If a Taskr’s Connect account later can’t receive transfers, the Taskr’s payout may be delayed until onboarding is finished; the Seeker’s payment stays in escrow meanwhile. We may show an in-app message asking the Taskr to finish onboarding.

8. Cancellations and refunds

  1. Before a request: Seekers may cancel eligible open/draft listings per in-app rules.
  2. Pending requests (awaiting payment): a Taskr can withdraw and a Seeker can decline a request before payment, and unpaid requests expire after 24 hours. No one is charged and the listing reopens.
  3. Seeker cancels 24 hours or more before the scheduled start: the Seeker gets a full refund. 1-hour grace period: a Seeker who cancels within 1 hour of paying also gets a full refund, with no late fee, as long as the scheduled start hasn’t passed. This applies even if the start is less than 24 hours away. The listing then closes.
  4. Seeker cancels less than 24 hours before the scheduled start: unless the 1-hour grace period in item 3 applies, the Seeker is refunded the price minus a late cancellation fee of 20% of the price, up to $25. The Taskr receives 90% of the late fee, and Taskr keeps 10%. The Taskr’s share is released 48 hours after the cancellation, unless either party opens a dispute during that window. For example, on a $60 task the late fee is $12: the Seeker gets $48 back and the Taskr receives $10.80. On a $200 task the fee is capped at $25: the Seeker gets $175 back and the Taskr receives $22.50. The listing then closes.
  5. After the scheduled start: the Seeker can no longer cancel. If something goes wrong, open a dispute (Section 7), or use Taskr didn’t show if item 7 applies.
  6. Taskr cancels: if the Taskr cancels a booking at any time, the Seeker gets a full refund, with no late fee. The listing goes back on the board as Open automatically. If a Taskr cancels 3 bookings within 30 days, their account is flagged for review. A no-show (item 7) counts as a cancellation for this purpose. If the Seeker asked the Taskr in the booking chat to cancel, Taskr support can clear that cancellation so it doesn’t count. To ask, email support@taskrak.com.
  7. Taskr no-show: if the Taskr hasn’t marked the job as started 2 hours after the scheduled start, a Taskr didn’t show button appears for the Seeker in the app. It stays until the Taskr marks the job as started. If the Seeker taps it, the booking is cancelled and the Seeker gets an instant full refund, with no late fee. The listing goes back on the board as Open automatically. The refund stands even if the Taskr marks the job as started afterward. If the Taskr marks the job as started before the Seeker taps the button, the button goes away, and the Seeker can still open a dispute (Section 7).
  8. Changing the time: the scheduled start can’t be changed after payment. To change it, either party cancels under the rules above. If the Taskr cancels, the listing goes back on the board and the Taskr can request it again with the new time. If the Seeker cancels, the listing closes, and the Seeker posts the task again.
  9. Account deletion: if the Seeker or the Taskr deletes their account, each of their paid bookings is handled this way:
    • if the Taskr hasn’t marked the job as started, the booking is cancelled and the Seeker gets a full refund, with no late fee, no matter which side deleted;
    • if the job has started but isn’t confirmed complete, the held payment stays frozen until Taskr support decides whether to refund the Seeker or release the payment to the Taskr (Section 7); and
    • if the job is confirmed complete, or treated as confirmed, the payment is released to the Taskr as usual. Payouts go to the Taskr’s own Stripe account, which the Taskr can still reach through Stripe after deleting their Taskr account.
    If the Seeker deletes their account, their listings close. If the Taskr deletes their account, any listing they were booked on goes back on the board as Open.
  10. Banned accounts: if an account is banned, its bookings and held payments are handled as described in Section 2.5.
  11. Disputes: in any other case, whether you get a refund depends on the outcome of the dispute (Section 7).
  12. Chargebacks: if a card chargeback is filed, any held payment that hasn’t been released yet is frozen while we review it. Chargebacks filed in bad faith may lead to account suspension.

Card refunds may take 5–10 business days to appear on your statement.
